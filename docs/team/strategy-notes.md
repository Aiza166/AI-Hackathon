# Win by Letting Judges Break It

The way to stand out at Coder's Cup 2026 is to build one narrow, working "last mile" flow and make every required property something a judge can trigger and check on the spot. The flow: a BISP beneficiary, or a helper acting for her, checks a suspicious "relief payment" SMS. Jev then routes the case to auto-handle, ask-for-proof, or a human. Judges should be able to trigger "I am not sure", withdraw consent, publish a new rule version, throttle the network and replay the audit trail themselves, live. Anything left on a slide is weaker. This fits the brief's "behavior over claims" scoring and the way experienced judges score: one clear flow understood in about 90 seconds beats five half-features ([JetBrains](https://blog.jetbrains.com/ai/2026/06/how-to-win-a-hackathon-notes-from-the-judging-table/)). Jev suits the job because it returns calibrated, typed probabilities rather than text, at **$0.042 per million input tokens** with free output ([TypeSafe Models](https://docs.typesafe.ai/models.md)). It has three gaps the team must design around. It has no native abstain answer. Its Urdu and Roman Urdu support is undocumented. It reads adversarial text at face value, and scam messages are adversarial text ([Model jaggedness](https://docs.typesafe.ai/model-jaggedness.md)). The stack should be FastAPI, Supabase Postgres, GoRules ZEN for versioned rules, and a Vite React PWA on always-on hosting. On the team's final-year project (FYP), the recommendation is light disclosure: one line and a provenance table, organizer clearance in writing before coding starts, and thresholds re-fitted during the event. Omitting the FYP saves almost nothing and carries the worst downside.

## Judges reward a 90-second "this works now" moment and punish cosmetic governance

General judging advice and govtech-specific advice point the same way. Judges reward one clear problem, a demo they understand quickly, and honesty about limits. A strong project with a confusing demo loses to a simpler one the judges follow ([JetBrains](https://blog.jetbrains.com/ai/2026/06/how-to-win-a-hackathon-notes-from-the-judging-table/)). Pakistan's own Code for Pakistan "AI for Civic Innovation" hackathon scored **civic and social impact, effective use of AI, feasibility, UX and communication** alongside technical implementation ([Devpost](https://ai-for-civic-innovation.devpost.com/)). So the pitch should open with a person in a hard moment, not with architecture. Government evaluators go further. Federal AI hackathons require **audit trails and human-in-the-loop controls** ([Maximus](https://maximus.com/insights/government-technology-adoption-federal-ai-hackathon)). One govtech practitioner says most govtech AI demos would fail a county audit in ten minutes because they lack plain-language rationale, data lineage, human override and an audit trail ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/cynthiaiblack_most-govtech-ai-demos-would-fail-a-county-activity-7389277418815033344-LHc7)). A bare "human in the loop" label also does not count as governance. Real governance means published thresholds and escalation rules ([FedScoop](https://fedscoop.com/human-in-the-loop-ai-governance/); [Nominal](https://nominal.so/blog/human-in-the-loop-ai-governance-accounting/)). The practical conclusion is that the human handoff needs visible numbers, and the audit log must record AI actions, not only human ones.

The demo choreography follows from that. Before building, write four lines for each challenge: the claim, the evidence, the action on screen, and the backup if the live environment fails. Build the "golden path" first: one realistic input, one meaningful action, one clearly better outcome ([Chinonso Ani](https://chinonsoani.com/blog/how-to-win-hackathons/)). The persona hook is grounded in fact. BISP officials state that **all BISP messages come from 8171 only** ([The Nation](https://www.nation.com.pk/12-Jan-2024/people-warned-of-fraud-in-bisp-name)), so a fake "relief payment" SMS from an 11-digit number asking for a fee is a realistic, sourced scenario. The recommended slot plan below is the team's own synthesis. No source covered "hand the judge the controls" panels, but the brief's scoring makes them the strongest available proof.

| Time | Segment | What the judge sees |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:45 | Persona hook | Widow receives fake BISP SMS on a weak link; 8171 fact on screen |
| 0:45–2:30 | Golden path | Helper submits the SMS. Jev's typed answers feed a code-side router. Citizen gets an icon, a traffic-light verdict and Urdu audio; officer gets a trace |
| 2:30–6:00 | Judge breaks it | Judge console: go offline, inject "Mohammad vs Muhammad" duplicate, publish rule v2, withdraw consent, upload forged CNIC, send borderline text that must yield "I am not sure" |
| 6:00–7:30 | Auditor replay | Step through the exact events just triggered, with actor, role, rule version, model ID, probabilities; eval page with FPR/FNR/abstain rate |
| 7:30–end | Honest limits | What is mocked, measured false-alarm rate, next validation step |

Each of the eight challenges needs a number, a toggle and an audit entry. Consent must be enforced in API middleware, so a withdrawal makes the officer's next API call return 403 and the blocked attempt appears in the log. Rules must live in the database with a version, so a new case runs on v2 while an old case still shows v1. The most likely ways to lose are all avoidable: a chatbot wrapper (the brief says "not a chatbot"), consent enforced only in the UI, hardcoded rules, a claim of 100% accuracy, and a deployed link that is cold or down during judging. Live demos fail on venue internet, API outages and auth problems. A recorded backup video is the standard fix, and one team said it saved their presentation when the Wi-Fi dropped ([Rise In](https://www.risein.com/guide/hackathon-demo-day); [LinkedIn, LA Hacks](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/bill-zhang1_last-weekend-at-the-la-hacks-ai-hackathon-activity-7518688523294674944-LHc7)). Freeze features in the final quarter and test from a clean device ([Chinonso Ani](https://chinonsoani.com/blog/how-to-win-hackathons/)).

One uncertainty matters here. No post-mortems of earlier Coder's Cup events were found, and the judges' identities and weightings are unknown. The plan therefore targets the brief's stated criteria rather than any known panel preferences.

## Jev answers typed questions with probabilities, so "I am not sure" must live in your code

Jev is TypeSafe's hosted "System One" decision model. It takes a `state` (string, object or array) and named questions of four types and returns structured answers, never prose ([Docs index](https://docs.typesafe.ai/llms.txt)). The four types are Noul (yes/no), Choice (1–255 options), Score (1–10 ordered levels) and Box (image region). The current release is **`jev-1.14.0`** (released 2026-10-14), aliased as `jev-latest`. It is served at `POST https://api.typesafe.ai/v1/systemone` ([Models](https://docs.typesafe.ai/models.md)). Version 1.14 added "better multilingual support", the Box type and images in state ([Changelog](https://docs.typesafe.ai/changelog.md)). The response's `model` field names the versioned ID that actually answered and "may differ from the alias supplied". Pin `jev-1.14.0` and log that field on every decision ([Models](https://docs.typesafe.ai/models.md)). Jev can also be reached through Vercel's AI Gateway (`typesafe-ai/jev`, `POST /v1/decisions`, AI SDK `decide()`), which offers per-request zero data retention and opt-in fallbacks ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/docs/ai-gateway/modalities/decision)). OpenAI's Decisions API uses the same shape but serves `gpt-6-luna`, not Jev. It is the only one of the three with a Refusal answer type ([OpenAI guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/decisions)).

Abstention is the design issue that matters most for this brief. TypeSafe's schema has **no refusal type**. Every question gets a typed answer. A Choice always picks an option even when none fits, and Noul answers carry no confidence field ([API reference](https://docs.typesafe.ai/api/system-one.md)). The documented guidance gives the rest of the design. A Noul above 0.7 is yes, below 0.3 is no, and the band between is undecided. Thresholds should rise with risk, and the docs' example uses a 0.5 floor and 0.9 for high-stakes actions. `confidence` is a deterministic transform of the probabilities, not a separate estimate of correctness, and gating naively on it is called out as a common mistake ([Confidence](https://docs.typesafe.ai/confidence.md)). The guidance for 1.14 is to pair each Choice with a "companion Noul" asking whether any option fits, rather than adding a "none of the above" option ([Docs index](https://docs.typesafe.ai/llms.txt)). The "I am not sure" path should therefore be a typed `UNSURE` route computed in code. Trigger it when a Noul lands in the 0.3–0.7 band, when the companion Noul says no option fits, when atomic answers conflict, or when the action moves money. Store the reason code, raw probabilities and thresholds applied. That makes the handoff auditable rather than cosmetic.

The documented weaknesses shape how to frame the scam questions. TypeSafe lists literal reading, math, dates, multi-hop indirection, large irrelevant state, contradictory criteria and first-option bias among Jev's weak spots. Most relevant here, "state is data, and Jev does not treat it as hostile by default": injected instructions or text that argues for its own classification can shift the answer ([Model jaggedness](https://docs.typesafe.ai/model-jaggedness.md)). A scam SMS is exactly that kind of text. So do not ask "is this a scam?" Ask atomic, observable Nouls in one bundled call: does the message ask for an OTP or PIN, does it ask for a fee, does it claim to come from a government body, does it contain a link. Then combine the answers with deterministic facts (is the sender 8171, is the domain on `*.bisp.gov.pk`) in code. Keep all dates, income and PMT arithmetic out of Jev entirely. Bundling questions that share state into one request is efficient: questions run in parallel and the state is billed once.

| Limit | Value | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Price | $0.042/M input tokens, output free ([Models](https://docs.typesafe.ai/models.md)) | A ~432-token message costs about $0.000018; cost is irrelevant at demo scale |
| Context | 64k per request, 32k per question; 262k with long-context header, "slower and less accurate" ([Models](https://docs.typesafe.ai/models.md)) | Keep state small and filtered |
| Latency | Vendor claims 70–500 ms ([Launch blog](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev)); third-party p50 236–276 ms on 1.13 ([Laya](https://laya.studio/learn/what-is-jev)) | Fine for live demo; still add timeout and circuit breaker |
| Rate limits | Per organization, shown only in console ([Models](https://docs.typesafe.ai/models.md)) | Read them in hour one |
| Free credit | $5 reported ([CodeRabbit](https://www.coderabbit.ai/blog/the-merge-typesafe-jev-smart-if-statements)) but reportedly suspended for new sign-ups ([AI FrontPage](https://aifront-page.com/typesafe-ai-reopens-jev-sign-ups-free-credit-suspended/)) | Budget a small paid top-up |
| Languages | No published list; no Urdu benchmark ([Changelog](https://docs.typesafe.ai/changelog.md)) | Test Roman Urdu empirically on day one |

Calibration remains a vendor claim. No independent reliability study was found, so the team's own labeled eval set is the only evidence a judge should be shown. TypeSafe offers no official self-hosted version. Community replicas exist, including `jeff`, a 400M-parameter drop-in that works with the official SDK after a base-URL change ([GitHub](https://github.com/logan-markewich/jeff)), and larger replicas that need datacenter GPUs ([jev-agent.com](https://jev-agent.com/open-source)). They can serve as an offline fallback only if they are tagged as a different model in the audit log and their thresholds are recalibrated. Their licences and quality were not verified, and none of them should be presented as TypeSafe's product.

## Pakistan's 8171 rule, India's consent artefact and Estonia's data tracker give credible blueprints

The scam detector's strongest signals come from BISP's own trust anchor. Genuine messages come only from **8171**, eligibility is checked at 8171.bisp.gov.pk or by SMS to 8171, and the helpline is 0800-26477 ([The Nation](https://www.nation.com.pk/12-Jan-2024/people-warned-of-fraud-in-bisp-name); [bisp.gov.pk](https://www.bisp.gov.pk/); [8171 portal](https://8171.bisp.gov.pk/)). Reported scam patterns include fee, OTP or card-number requests, "processing fee" links, lookalike domains such as `bisp8171-payment.net`, copied official wording with the victim's CNIC inserted, and 11-digit sender numbers ([apkischeme.com](https://apkischeme.com/safety/avoid-bisp-scam-fake-sms/)). The stakes are sizeable. Secondary reporting puts NCCIA cybercrime complaints at **148,204 in 2026, up from 104,490**, with financial fraud the top category at 71,051 ([TechJuice via Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/techjuicepk/posts/online-fraud-tops-cybercrime-list-as-complaints-surge-by-tens-of-thousandspakist/1555395086616894/)). That figure was not confirmed against an NCCIA release, so label it as reported. BISP is piloting Raast-based payment and says beneficiary biometric verification "cannot be compromised" ([bisp.gov.pk](https://bisp.gov.pk/NewsDetail/OWQxYjQxYTEtNGZmMS00M2RlLWJlM2YtNmY2N2EyZTNjYzcx)). The prototype should therefore mock NADRA verification as an external attestation (match or no-match plus a transaction ID) and never store biometrics. The "safe" path should send users to official channels rather than process payments. Reported eligibility figures, such as a PMT cutoff of 32, come only from unofficial pages ([Facebook guide](https://www.facebook.com/8171BISPguide/posts/-stopped-payments-complete-your-nser-dynamic-registry-surveyif-your-bisp-install/1188752693670496/)). That makes them ideal examples for versioned rules (`2025-26-v1`) rather than hardcoded facts. The handoff screen can link to real reporting routes: the NCCIA complaint portal ([NCCIA](https://complaint.nccia.gov.pk/)) and PTA's fraud reporting page ([PTA](https://www.pta.gov.pk/category/report-fraud-callssmss-884766986-2023-05-30)). The PTA 8557 short code and the NCCIA helpline number were not confirmed and should not be quoted.

For consent, India's Account Aggregator **consent artefact** is the most defensible template to cite. It is machine-readable and signed, and it declares purpose, data fields, fetch frequency, data life and validity. It is revocable at any stage, and every consent event is logged and notified ([Sahamati](https://sahamati.org.in/what-is-consent-artefact/)). Each purpose needs its own artefact ([Sahamati FAQ](https://sahamati.org.in/faq/)). Sahamati's June 2026 agentic framework goes further and uses purpose codes and data-life fields to limit what an AI agent may do with consented data ([Sahamati PDF](https://sahamati.org.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/AA-AI-Agentic-Framework.pdf)). That maps closely onto "Jev may read this message only for scam screening." India's DoT Financial Fraud Risk Indicator, which tags phone numbers Medium, High or Very High risk and shares the tags with payment providers, reportedly prevented over Rs 5,000 crore of suspected fraud. Its tier vocabulary suits sender-risk labels ([DoT](https://www.dot.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/09/ee48cf6fd82141ca33fce2fe9f628973.pdf)). Estonia's **Data Tracker** shows citizens which institution accessed their data and why, and routes violations to the Data Protection Inspectorate ([e-Estonia](https://e-estonia.com/data-tracker-build-citizen-trust/)). A "Who saw my data" screen with a "Report misuse" button on each row turns the audit log into a citizen-facing feature.

Two further analogues frame the scam work. Singapore's ScamShield combines an AI classifier with human verification. It scans over 400,000 websites daily at **under 0.1% false positives** and has blacklisted over 120,000 scam entities ([GovTech Singapore](https://www.tech.gov.sg/products-and-services/for-citizens/scam-prevention/)). That is the model for "AI plus human review, with a published false-positive rate." Brazil's Pix MED shows that a claim-and-freeze path is "a recovery mechanism, not a reversal mechanism" ([PagBrasil](https://www.pagbrasil.com/blog/pix/med-2-0/)). UK Confirmation of Payee covers over 99% of Faster Payments ([PSR](https://www.psr.org.uk/news-and-updates/latest-news/news/anti-fraud-tool-confirmation-of-payee-expanded-to-hundreds-of-more-firms/)), but it is weak against scams where the victim is persuaded to pay ([BioCatch](https://www.biocatch.com/blog/confirmation-of-payee-effective-against-fraud)). That limitation is a good honest point to raise on the limits slide. Accessibility and South Asian name-matching guidance were not sourced this session. The team's designs there are sound engineering, but they should not be presented as established practice.

## A Python backend keeps Jev, rules, matching and tests in one language

FastAPI is the right backend for this team. Jev's Python SDK (`pip install typesafe-sdk`, `TypeSafeClient`, `Noul`/`Choice`/`Score`) ([Python SDK](https://docs.typesafe.ai/sdk/python.md)), GoRules ZEN, rapidfuzz, OCR and pytest are all Python-native, and FastAPI generates Swagger docs that show its security schemes ([FastAPI security](https://fastapi.tiangolo.com/tutorial/security/)). Those docs give the team a live surface for showing a citizen token getting 403 on an officer endpoint. **GoRules ZEN** stores rules as portable JSON decision models that can be loaded from a database, evaluates them in microseconds, and ships an open-source React editor ([gorules/zen](https://github.com/gorules/zen); [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/zen-engine/0.17.0/)). That combination delivers the "rules change without redeploy" challenge directly. Store each version as an immutable `rule_versions` row and save `rule_version_id` with every decision. A "simulate v2 on the last 200 cases" replay then becomes a cheap differentiator.

Hosting choices are where demos die. Render's free tier spins services down after 15 idle minutes and takes about a minute to wake. Its free Postgres expires after 30 days ([Render](https://render.com/docs/free)). Supabase Free pauses a project after a week of inactivity and caps the database at 500 MB ([Supabase pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing); [pausing](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/free-project-pausing)). Railway runs without sleeping for about $5 a month ([secondary](https://justinmckelvey.com/blog/is-render-free)). The backend should therefore run on Railway or Fly, the frontend on Vercel, and the database on a Supabase project created this week. Use Supabase Auth JWTs verified in a FastAPI dependency, plus hashed API keys. Enforce consent in one `assert_consent()` call that every read passes through. The backend's service role bypasses Supabase row-level security, so RLS can only be a second line of defence. The audit log should be an append-only table with UPDATE and DELETE revoked and a SHA-256 hash chain. A `/audit/verify` endpoint then lets judges confirm the chain is intact.

Voice needs care. Google STT V2 supports `ur-PK` with `chirp_3` ([Google STT](https://docs.cloud.google.com/speech-to-text/docs/speech-to-text-supported-languages)), but Google TTS lists only `ur-IN` voices ([Google TTS](https://docs.cloud.google.com/text-to-speech/docs/list-voices-and-types)). Azure has Pakistani voices, `ur-PK-UzmaNeural` and `ur-PK-AsadNeural` ([Azure](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/language-support)). ElevenLabs' multilingual v2 and Flash v2.5 models do not list Urdu ([ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/models)). The best open Urdu Whisper fine-tune self-reports about **26% WER** ([HF](https://huggingface.co/kingabzpro/whisper-large-v3-urdu)). Meta's MMS Urdu TTS is non-commercial ([HF](https://huggingface.co/facebook/mms-tts-urd-script_arabic)). The reliable demo design is pre-generated Azure audio for fixed prompts, cached in the PWA so it works offline. STT, if used at all, should only pre-fill fields that the user confirms with a tap. The offline PWA, the Dexie outbox, idempotency keys, OCR choices and forgery signals were not re-sourced this session. Treat them as engineering defaults: present forgery output as "risk indicators for officer review", never as a verdict, and do not rely on the Background Sync API, which is Chromium-only.

## Disclose the FYP lightly, clear it in writing, and re-measure during the event

The rules in this field draw the line at code and project-specific artifacts, not at ideas or knowledge. MLH's standard rules allow teams to work on an idea they have worked on before "as long as they do not re-use code or other project materials" ([MLH](https://github.com/MLH/mlh-policies/blob/main/standard-hackathon-rules.md)). ETHGlobal bars "pre-existing project-specific code, designs, or assets" ([ETHGlobal](https://ethglobal.com/events/istanbul/info/start)). Devfolio bars modified pre-existing projects and checks repo history ([Devfolio](https://guide.devfolio.co/docs/guide/participating-in-hackathons/judging-1)). The Coder's Cup wording ("all development must take place during the hackathon... pre-built projects are prohibited") sits at the strict end. Under it, the FYP's **fitted threshold values and normaliser word lists are the grey zone**. They are project materials, not general knowledge, so retyping them into fresh code is the act most likely to be read as reuse. The measured FP/FN rates are research results. Citing them is normal citation, but presenting them as measured during the event would break the brief's own "tell the judges what is real and what is simulated" rule.

The precedents favour disclosure. At HackMIT 2014, team AgileAssault "wrote different code but used the same underlying idea" as a project already judged at another event and lost its top-eight placement. A second team stepped down after failing to attribute the Microsoft Research work it built on, which one member later called plagiarism ([The Tech](https://www.thetech.com/2014/11/07/hackmit-v134-n53); [The Tech](https://thetech.com/2014/11/14/hackmit-v134-n54)). At the 2013 Salesforce hackathon, undisclosed prior work produced a fairness dispute after the result, and Salesforce paid a second $1M first prize. The review found that judges "may not have been provided with enough information" ([Network World](https://www.networkworld.com/article/683788/mobile-apps-salesforce-com-completes-hackathon-review-awards-second-1-million-prize.html)). Domain depth can also win outright. Third place at Anthropic's "no pre-existing work" hackathon went to a cardiologist who built a new product extending his deployed earlier one ([Claude blog](https://claude.com/resources/articles/meet-the-winners-of-our-built-with-opus-4-6-claude-code-hackathon)). Pakistani faculty judges will also know HEC's definition of self-plagiarism: re-using significant portions of one's own work without citing the original ([DUET policy](https://www.duet.edu.pk/media/DUET-Plagiarism-Policy.pdf)).

| | A: Full disclosure | B: Omit entirely | C: Light disclosure (recommended) |
|---|---|---|---|
| What judges hear | FYP presented as the foundation | Nothing | One line plus a labeled provenance table |
| Rule fit | Compliant if no code is reused, but framing reads as "pre-built" | Breaks "real vs simulated" if any FYP number or artifact appears uncited | Directly satisfies "tell the judges what is real" |
| Credibility of error rates | High | Thin numbers measured in 48h only | High: prior study and today's figures side by side |
| Originality score | Risk of "FYP 2.0" discount | Looks new | Neutral if the pitch leads with what is new |
| Discovery risk | None | Highest: supervisor, expo, LinkedIn, GitHub | None |
| Worst case | Build scored as incremental | Disqualification or public dispute | A follow-up question answered from `PRIOR_WORK.md` |

Option C wins on every row except a marginal "looks new" edge for B. That edge disappears the moment anyone at a Karachi university event recognizes the FYP, and local faculty and industry judges are plausibly more likely to recognize it than judges at an international event. Three safeguards make C safe. First, email the organizers now, before writing code, describing the FYP and the plan to write everything fresh, and keep the reply. Second, re-fit thresholds and re-measure FP/FN inside the hackathon repo with the fitting script committed. Third, keep the FYP to one spoken line, one slide footnote and a README section, and lead the pitch with the new product. Re-fitting is required on technical grounds too. Jev 1.14 was released on 2026-10-14 with changed multilingual behaviour ([Changelog](https://docs.typesafe.ai/changelog.md)), so thresholds fitted on an earlier version are stale either way. That makes "prior study vs today on jev-1.14.0" an honest reproducibility check rather than a head start. If the organizers object in writing to any mention of the FYP, fall back to B, with no FYP number or artifact anywhere in the project.

## Conclusion

The brief's phrase "silent guessing earns zero" lines up with Jev's main design gap. Jev never refuses and always answers in type, so a team that trusts its outputs directly will guess silently by construction. The winning move is to treat Jev as a sensor and keep the decision in code. Jev answers small, observable questions with calibrated probabilities. Versioned rules, explicit bands and consent checks then decide what happens, and every step lands in a hash-chained log that judges can replay. That architecture answers the responsible-AI and technical-judgment criteria at the same time. The FYP question turns out to be less about risk than about evidence. Jev 1.14 changed under the team's feet a month ago, so FYP thresholds are stale regardless of the rules.
